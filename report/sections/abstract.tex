We examine whether estimating asset-specific uncertainty improves portfolio decisions. Replication of enhanced portfolio optimization raises the gross Sharpe ratio from 0.32 to 0.77. With known uncertainty, restricting adjustment to one parameter costs 12--29\% of optimal utility. Controlled estimation errors can eliminate that advantage. Historically, common volatility targeting yields incremental annual net utility of +18 and $-44$ bp, with both confidence intervals including zero. Original uncertainty assignments outperform random assignments in the earlier fixed-risk-aversion comparison, but the advantage weakens under common position sizing. The results support a scalar baseline in this application while identifying when accurate asset-specific estimates can improve decisions.
