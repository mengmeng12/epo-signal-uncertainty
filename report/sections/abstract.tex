We examine when a common uncertainty adjustment is sufficient for portfolio choice. Replication recovers the performance improvement from enhanced portfolio optimization. Analytical conditions describe when scalar allocations approach the known-input optimum, while simulations quantify losses from heterogeneous uncertainty. Historical portfolios use bootstrap estimates of relative uncertainty. Annual net certainty equivalent differences are 28 basis points in 1970--2018 and negative 99 in 2019--2025; both confidence intervals include zero. Exposure targeting improves absolute utility for both strategies. The evidence supports retaining a common adjustment as a practical baseline while incremental net benefit from the additional uncertainty estimation remains unestablished.
