We study when a common uncertainty adjustment is sufficient for portfolio choice. Replication of enhanced portfolio optimization raises the gross Sharpe ratio from 0.32 to 0.77. With known inputs, the scalar restriction costs 12--29\% of mean optimal utility in two simulated profiles. Estimated heterogeneity yields annual net certainty equivalent differences of +28 bp in 1970--2018 and $-99$ bp in 2019--2025; both 95\% intervals include zero. The earlier advantage reverses at higher trading costs. A common adjustment remains a practical baseline: heterogeneous uncertainty matters in the model, but its estimated implementation delivers no consistent net improvement across periods and cost settings.
